02

TURN A CLAIM INTO A TEST

Reproducible
fixture

Reduce a reported behavior to a controlled input. Give the next engineer something concrete to rerun and challenge.

See the approachPrompt Boundary / paired readers ↗Afterimage / before and after ↗

Scope
One failure hypothesis, an agreed runtime, and a controlled set of inputs and reference outcomes.
You receive
Source code, a reproduction command, controls, result records, and regression checks for the agreed behavior.
Acceptance
The documented command produces the recorded result in the agreed environment. A benign control and relevant failure paths are covered.
03

MAKE THE MECHANISM LEGIBLE

Interactive
research demo

Turn a calculation, state transition, or research result into an interface people can operate—not just a slide they can look at.

Scope
One technical story, its source material, the interaction to explain it, and the intended audience.
You receive
A responsive browser demo, readable source, linked evidence, and a short explanation of what the model represents.
Acceptance
Inputs map to documented outputs. The agreed views work on desktop and mobile, keyboard controls are usable, and illustrative data is labeled.

02 / HOW IT STARTS

Agree on
“done.”

A good brief says what the result needs to make possible. It also names what the project will leave for a later pass.

Read my research method ↗
  1. 01

    Send the question and the material.

    Describe the component or idea, its audience, and the decision you need to make. Include a shareable source link and a deadline if one exists.

  2. 02

    Define the deliverable.

    We identify the exact surface, environment, evidence, and acceptance criteria. The proposal names the fee and schedule for that scope.

  3. 03

    Review the work against the brief.

    The handoff includes the agreed source and results, run instructions, dependencies, and any open limitations. Additional work gets its own scope.
